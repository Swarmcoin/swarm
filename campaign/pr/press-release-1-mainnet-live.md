# Press release 1 — for 7 October 2026

**FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE**

## SWARM mainnet is live: a proof-of-work privacy coin with shielded payments, an encrypted messenger that pays inside the chat, and a browser with the wallet built in. Public mining opens 1 November 2026.

*7 October 2026* — SWARM (ticker SWM), an open-source privacy coin, has been running on its own
proof-of-work mainnet since 2 October 2026, 15:42 UTC. Shielded SWARM payments keep the
sender, the receiver and the amount encrypted on the chain while the network verifies them.
The project publishes a desktop and Android wallet, an end-to-end encrypted messenger with
payments inside the conversation, and a Windows pre-release of a browser with the wallet in
the toolbar. Mining opens to everyone on 1 November 2026, 15:42 UTC.

**What SWARM is.** SWARM is built on the Zcash protocol stack as implemented by the Zebra
full node. The consensus rules and the cryptography are unmodified; SWARM defines its own
network, its emission schedule and its allocation, and builds the applications around it.
The chain uses Equihash 200,9 proof of work with a 75-second target block time, a block
reward of 6.25 SWM that halves every 1,680,000 blocks, and a maximum supply of
20,999,987.3152 SWM. The genesis block holds no spendable coins.

**Where the coins go.** Every block pays 80% to the miner who found it and 20% to three
published addresses, for the whole life of the chain: 8% Core Development, 4% Grants &
Ecosystem and 8% Community & Development Reserve. The allocation is fixed in the genesis
rules, paid block by block in public, and is not a premine. The project states plainly that
the allocation is permanent and does not describe the launch as one without allocation.

**The closed start.** Until 31 October 2026, 15:42 UTC, only the project's own machines mine.
In those 29 days about 33,408 blocks and about 208,800 SWM are produced, about 0.99% of the
maximum supply, split like every other block. For the following 24 hours the people on the
public waiting list can mine; from 1 November 2026, 15:42 UTC mining is open to everyone and
the SWARM Node software, which runs a full node and mines on the CPU, is published with its
checksum. The published reason: the infrastructure around the coin, liquidity pools
included, needs coins before public mining begins. No coins are promised to anyone.

**The software.**
- *SWARM Wallet* 0.1.0-mainnet.11 for Windows, macOS and Linux, and an Android APK: shielded
  or transparent payments, a 24-word recovery phrase, a lock code. Based on Zingo.
- *SWARM Messenger* 0.1.5 for Windows, macOS and Linux: end-to-end encrypted messages and
  calls between SWARM wallets, sign-in with the 24 words instead of a phone number, payments
  inside the chat. Based on Signal's open-source code, running on SWARM's own server.
- *SWARM Browser* 153.0.8010.52-6, Windows pre-release: Chromium 153 from the
  ungoogled-chromium code base with Google's services removed, privacy defaults switched on
  and the SWARM wallet in the toolbar. Tracker blocking and paying websites are not in it yet.
- A public block explorer at mainnet.explore.swarm.green and a light-wallet SDK so other
  wallets can be built against the chain.

Every download on swarm.green carries its SHA-256 checksum. All code is open source under
MIT, Apache-2.0, AGPL-3.0 and BSD-3-Clause licences at github.com/Swarmcoin.

**Honest statement of the current state.** SWARM is experimental software. No independent
audit of the SWARM-specific changes has been published; the upstream components have their
own security records. Builds are not yet signed with a vendor certificate. Shielding
protects what is written to the chain, not the fact that a user connects to the network.

**Quote.** "Most digital money is a public record of who paid whom, how much and when. We
built the opposite, on the most reviewed shielded-payment system there is, and we changed
nothing in it. What we add is a network, a fixed allocation that we describe exactly as it
is, and the apps people need to use private money: a wallet, a messenger and a browser." —
the SWARM founding team.
