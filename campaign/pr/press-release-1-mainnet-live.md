## Money has become a record of you. SWARM is private money you can mine yourself, and public mining opens on 1 November.

### A proof-of-work privacy coin whose payments stay encrypted on the chain, a messenger that pays inside the conversation, and a browser with the wallet built in. Experimental, open source, and candid about what is unfinished.

*26 October 2026* — Every card payment, bank transfer and payment-app transaction is a record:
who paid whom, how much, when, and often where. Most cryptocurrencies make the record worse,
because a public ledger shows every payment to everyone, forever. Messages are no better once
a provider can read them or sell who talks to whom. SWARM (ticker SWM) was built against that
record. It is a cryptocurrency on its own proof-of-work blockchain whose shielded payments keep
the sender, the receiver and the amount encrypted on the chain while every node still verifies
that the payment is valid. The mainnet has been running since 2 October 2026, 15:42 UTC. On
1 November 2026, 15:42 UTC, mining opens to everyone.

**What is live today.** SWARM Wallet (Windows, macOS, Linux, Android) sends and receives SWM,
shielded by default, with a 24-word recovery phrase and a lock code. SWARM Messenger (Windows,
macOS, Linux) is end-to-end encrypted messaging between SWARM wallets, with payments inside the
chat; the sign-in is the wallet's 24 words, not a phone number, and it runs on SWARM's own
server. SWARM Browser (Windows pre-release) is a web browser built from the open-source Chromium
code with Google's services removed, privacy defaults switched on and the SWARM wallet in the
toolbar. A public block explorer shows every block and every allocation at
mainnet.explore.swarm.green. Every download on swarm.green carries its SHA-256 checksum.

**What is unfinished, in the project's own words.** SWARM is experimental software. No
independent audit of the SWARM-specific changes has been published; the components it builds
on have their own public security records. Builds are not yet signed with a vendor certificate.
The browser has no tracker blocking yet, and no macOS or Linux build. The messenger is a first
release with no phone app. Shielding protects what is written to the chain, not the fact that a
computer connects to the network. The project lists what is missing on every download page.

**Where the coins come from and where they go.** SWM is issued only by mining: a block about
every 75 seconds, 6.25 SWM per block, halving every 1,680,000 blocks, at most 20,999,987.3152
SWM. The supply follows the same shape as Bitcoin's, and for the same reason: money whose
supply anyone can compute in advance and nobody can change. The genesis block holds no spendable
coins; there was no premine, no sale, no VC round and no token offering. Every block pays 80% to
the miner who found it and 20% to three published project addresses for the whole life of the
chain: 8% Core Development, 4% Grants & Ecosystem, 8% Community & Development Reserve. The split
is fixed in the genesis rules, visible in every block, and cannot be changed. The project calls
it what it is: a permanent allocation, paid in public.

**The closed start.** Until 31 October 2026, 15:42 UTC only the project's own machines mine. In
those 29 days about 33,408 blocks and about 208,800 SWM are produced, about 0.99% of the maximum
supply, each block split like every other. For the following 24 hours the people on the public
waiting list can mine; from 1 November 2026, 15:42 UTC anyone can. The stated reason is an
orderly start: the infrastructure around the coin needs coins before public mining begins. The
closed start is documented block by block in the explorer. No coins are promised.

**Mining.** The proof of work is Equihash 200,9, left unchanged from the open-source
foundations SWARM builds on. At the opening the project publishes SWARM Node, a desktop
application for Windows, Linux and macOS (Apple silicon and Intel) that runs a full node, checks
every block from genesis, and mines on the CPU for as long as the user has pressed Start.
Specialised hardware for this proof of work exists and nothing in the rules keeps larger miners
out; the project states that trade-off instead of hiding it.
Please keep ASICs and rented hash power off the network.
